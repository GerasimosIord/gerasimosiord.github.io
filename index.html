About

Who I Am

I'm a biology undergraduate at the University of Patras, completing a thesis on gut microbiome dynamics in hematopoietic stem cell transplantation patients. My work is fundamentally statistical — using mixed models, diversity metrics, and longitudinal analysis to trace how the human microbiome collapses and sometimes rebuilds after one of medicine's most extreme interventions.

Before my thesis, I spent two months as a bioinformatics intern at ΕΚΕΒΕ "Alexander Fleming", where I built R analysis pipelines across 797 clinical samples. I'm drawn to causal inference as a framework for moving beyond association — understanding not just what correlates with disease, but what drives it. I've also co-authored a book chapter on nutrigenomics and neurological disorders.

Outside the lab, I think a lot about the philosophy of scientific discovery, the limits of observational data, and what it takes to make a causal claim in biology. I'm actively looking toward an MSc in Biostatistics.

Active · BSc Thesis 2024–2025 Oxford Nanopore · Full-length 16S rRNA · R

Gut Microbiome Dynamics in HSCT Patients

Tracking the collapse and potential recovery of the human gut microbiome following hematopoietic stem cell transplantation. The sequencing is full-length 16S rRNA via Oxford Nanopore; the analysis is statistical — mixed-effects models for longitudinal diversity, generalized linear models for pathobiont abundance, and DAG-guided reasoning about whether microbial shifts are mediators or consequences of clinical outcomes. The findings reveal dramatic disruption: near-total diversity loss driven by antibiotic conditioning and immunosuppression, with opportunistic pathogens rapidly filling the ecological vacuum.
